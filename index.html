Curator’s Synthesis

Chairs historically functioned as instruments of class, gender, and economic dominance, shaped directly by architecture, clothing, and manufacturing innovations. Rulers utilized monumental architectural forms, sacred carvings, and rare imports to visually elevate authority above ground-level laborers, while court protocols strictly codified rank through seat types. Concurrently, vestimentary demands and patriarchal norms sculpted chair form—narrowing backrests and flaring seat pans to enforce rigid modesty on corseted women, while male designs offered sprawling ease. Technological shifts from steam-bent wood to tubular steel and molded plastics ultimately democratized design, transforming an exclusive aristocratic luxury into an accessible global commodity.

— Anamika Nandi
